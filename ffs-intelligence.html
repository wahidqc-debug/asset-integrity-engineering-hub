Fitness-for-Service Intelligence

Pre-assessment data preparation and metal-loss screening. Not an API 579 acceptance assessment.

Assessment pathway

Part 4 — General Metal Loss

Collect representative thickness readings, original geometry, material, design and operating loads, required thickness, future corrosion allowance and inspection uncertainty. Confirm applicability and thickness averaging requirements before assessment.

Part 5 — Local Metal Loss

Collect longitudinal and circumferential thickness profiles, flaw dimensions, minimum thickness, proximity to discontinuities and future corrosion allowance. A single minimum reading cannot establish remaining strength.

Part 6 — Pitting Corrosion

Characterize pit depths, distribution, spacing, affected area and interaction with other damage. Confirm applicable assessment procedure and inspection coverage.

Other damage

Crack-like flaws, hydrogen damage, creep, distortion and brittle fracture require their own applicable assessment procedures. Do not substitute metal-loss calculations.

Metal loss screening calculator

Remaining Strength Factor (RSF)

RSF describes the ratio of the limit load of a damaged component to that of the corresponding undamaged component. It is not generally equal to the ratio of measured to nominal thickness. This prototype does not calculate RSF or allowable pressure for damaged components.

Assessment levels

Level 1: simplified assessment within specified applicability limits. Level 2: more detailed assessment using additional data. Level 3: advanced analysis, potentially including numerical methods. The appropriate level depends on the applicable procedure and available information.
