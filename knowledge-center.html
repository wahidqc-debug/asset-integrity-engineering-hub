Damage Mechanisms

API 571

Screening workflow

Identify material, process chemistry, phase, operating and excursion temperatures, stress, fabrication history and insulation. Screen internal and external environments separately. Confirm credible mechanisms using morphology and inspection evidence.

Mechanism families

General and localized thinning: CO₂ corrosion, oxygen corrosion, MIC, erosion-corrosion and CUI. Cracking: chloride SCC, wet H₂S damage, fatigue and creep-related cracking. Metallurgical degradation: HTHA, embrittlement and graphitization. Applicability depends on material and exposure.

Inspection selection

UT mapping for thinning; PT or MT for accessible surface cracks as appropriate to material; qualified volumetric techniques for internal cracking. A negative result is meaningful only within demonstrated coverage and detection capability.

Example

Wet carbon steel production piping may experience CO₂ corrosion and localized attack at water hold-up locations. Verify water chemistry, CO₂ partial pressure, flow regime and inhibitor performance before selecting inspection locations.

Corrosion & Materials

API 510 / 570; API 571

Corrosion rate

LTCR = (initial thickness − latest thickness) / elapsed years. STCR = (previous thickness − latest thickness) / elapsed years. Compare the same TML and measurement basis; investigate apparent thickness gain and uncertainty.

Remaining life

RL = (actual thickness − required thickness) / representative positive corrosion rate. A zero rate does not establish infinite life. At or below required thickness triggers engineering review, not automatic leakage.

Material selection

Carbon steel relies on corrosion allowance, chemistry control or protection. Stainless steel requires chloride and temperature review; duplex requires phase balance and welding control; Cu-Ni seawater service requires velocity, water chemistry and commissioning review. GRE requires resin compatibility and pressure-temperature qualification.

Worked example

Initial 12 mm, previous 11 mm at year 4, latest 10.4 mm at year 6. LTCR = 0.267 mm/y; STCR = 0.30 mm/y. If 0.30 is justified and required thickness is 8 mm, projected RL = 8 years.

Monitoring

Use repeatable TMLs, calibrated instruments, corrosion coupons or probes where appropriate, process chemistry, inhibitor records and IOW excursions. Localized corrosion requires spatial coverage rather than a single average rate.

Risk-Based Inspection

API 580 / 581

Framework

Define equipment boundaries, credible damage mechanisms, inspection history, operating conditions and consequence scenarios. Risk combines probability and consequence; remaining life is not a risk category.

Quantitative model

Failure frequency = generic failure frequency × damage factor × management systems factor, using applicable API 581 rules and units. Risk is frequency multiplied by compatible consequence. Use edition-specific component and release scenario data.

Inspection effectiveness

Select effectiveness based on mechanism, technique, coverage and quality, not simply the number of inspections. Combine damage factors using applicable methodology rather than arbitrary summation.

Example

Two components with identical thinning rates can have different risk due to inventory, isolation, release consequences and inspection evidence. Prioritize using validated risk drivers and approved risk criteria.

Fitness-for-Service

API 579-1 / ASME FFS-1

Assessment selection

Part 3 brittle fracture; Part 4 general metal loss; Part 5 local metal loss; Part 6 pitting; Part 7 hydrogen damage; Part 9 crack-like flaws; Part 10 creep; Part 12 dents and gouges. Confirm applicability and exclusions.

Assessment levels

Level 1 generally uses simplified conservative screening; Level 2 more detailed inputs and analysis; Level 3 advanced assessment by suitably qualified specialists. Exact requirements vary by part.

Required evidence

Design code, geometry, material properties, loads, operating envelope, flaw dimensions and inspection uncertainty. For local metal loss, collect thickness profiles and establish future corrosion allowance as required.

Decision

A thickness below required design thickness warrants evaluation; it does not establish immediate failure or acceptable operation. Document restrictions, monitoring and reassessment conditions through authorized engineering review.

Inspection & NDT

API 510 / 570 / 653; ASME Section V

Method selection

VT identifies visible deterioration; UT measures thickness and can detect volumetric flaws with qualified procedures; MT detects surface and near-surface flaws in ferromagnetic materials; PT detects surface-breaking flaws; RT images suitable volumetric discontinuities.

Planning

Define expected damage, susceptible locations, required coverage, access, surface condition, detection capability and acceptance basis. PAUT, TOFD and AUT require suitable qualification and geometry review.

Data quality

Record tag, location, date, method, calibration, thickness or flaw dimensions, limitations and inspector identification. Compare repeat readings at equivalent locations.

Intervals

Use applicable inspection code, approved RBI and site requirements. Distinguish scheduled dates from completed inspection compliance and avoid treating calculated remaining life as an automatic inspection interval.

Case Studies

Illustrative examples

Accelerated thinning

10 mm initially, 9 mm at year 5, 8.5 mm at year 6. LTCR = 0.25 mm/y; STCR = 0.50 mm/y. Investigate process changes and measurement uncertainty before choosing a projection rate.

Lined separator

Intact lining and no observed substrate corrosion do not justify automatically transferring piping corrosion rates to the vessel. Review lining condition, holidays, service history and uncertainty; establish a documented representative rate.

Seawater cooler

Elevated velocity may contribute to erosion-corrosion in Cu-Ni tubing. Verify actual flow distribution, inlet turbulence, material, protective film and water quality; inspect inlet regions and assess design limits.

Below required thickness

A pipe at 7.8 mm against required 8.0 mm needs engineering review. Determine damage morphology, loads, measurement uncertainty and applicable FFS procedure. Do not infer leakage or safe continued service from thickness alone.
